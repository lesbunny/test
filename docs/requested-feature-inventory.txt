E. WEAPON ROLL APPRAISAL

What it evaluates
- The specific perks on a weapon.
- Community-recommended perk combinations.
- Whether a curator explicitly marks a roll as undesirable.
- Enhanced versions of perks and their base equivalents.
- The weapon's gear tier.
- The weapon's power.
- Other copies of the same weapon in the collection.
- Whether another copy is a stronger candidate to keep.

Verdict and rating behavior
- God roll: a match to a recommended roll.
- Keeper: an item worth retaining according to its appraisal.
- Filler: a lower-priority item.
- Shard: a strong candidate for dismantling review.
- Letter grades range from S+ through D in the documented grading system.
- Wishlist match: identify the recommendation or curator associated with a roll.
- Trash-roll flag: honor explicit negative recommendations where applicable.
- Enhanced-perk normalization: avoid treating an enhanced perk as an entirely unrelated perk when checking a wishlist.
- Refreshed community wishlist data can influence future appraisals after syncing.

Duplicate ranking
1. Evaluate copies against relevant roll recommendations.
2. Consider gear-tier differences.
3. Compare power.
4. Rank competing copies.
5. Identify the strongest keeper candidate.
6. Flag inferior duplicates for review.
7. Re-evaluate rankings after relevant changes, such as locking or infusing an item and syncing again.

The aim is to determine which actual copy deserves a vault slot rather than labeling every copy independently.

F. ARMOR 3.0 APPRAISAL

Armor analysis
- Read the armor archetype from game data.
- Read artifice slots and stat-tuning options.
- Account for tuning changes when calculating possible stat distributions.
- Compare pieces on a common, assumed-masterworked basis.
- Evaluate attainable stat spreads instead of considering only the displayed total.
- Identify armor that another owned piece dominates.
- Evaluate combinations of owned armor when determining whether an item is redundant.
- Consider class items and relevant set bonuses.
- Consider exotic class-item perk combinations when evaluating value.
- Recognize event armor whose set bonus depends on a socketed selector.
- Recheck rankings after relevant item changes and a subsequent sync.

Example
Two helmets may have different displayed totals. The one with a lower total may have useful tuning flexibility or an artifice slot. The app attempts to determine whether the lower-total helmet can achieve a stat distribution that the other helmet cannot match. An armor piece is meant to be marked redundant only when owned alternatives dominate the distributions it can achieve. This is more sophisticated than sorting armor only from lowest total to highest.

G. THE SHARD LIST — RANKED VAULT CLEANUP

The Shard List converts appraisal results into an actionable, prioritized cleanup queue.

List behavior
- Display items identified as shard candidates.
- Rank items rather than listing them alphabetically.
- Prioritize explicit trash-roll findings and redundant armor.
- Consider verdict, gear tier, and power.
- Rank inferior copies below the best available copy.
- Exclude gear referenced by saved Shard or Keep loadouts.
- Protect gear referenced by supported in-game loadouts.
- Exclude locked items.
- Exclude high-kill favorites from applicable cleanup workflows.
- Review candidates one at a time.
- Confirm multiple suggestions together.
- Stop once enough space has been reclaimed.

Keyboard-based review
- Use arrow keys to navigate candidates.
- Press 1 to keep an item.
- Press 3 to confirm a shard decision.
- Review recommendations without opening each item's detail dialog.
- The New Drops interface uses a similar quick-review style.

Safety boundaries
- The Shard List is a recommendation and staging system, not an automatic dismantler.
- It does not destroy gear.
- It protects items used by saved builds.
- It respects locking and applicable favorite protections.
- It helps prepare confirmed junk for manual dismantling inside the game.
- Confirming an appraisal is not the same operation as dismantling an item.

H. BULK ACTIONS AND GEAR HOUSEKEEPING

1. Move unequipped gear to the vault
- Select the account housekeeping operation.
- Move eligible unequipped weapons and armor off characters.
- Leave equipped gear in place.
- Leave Postmaster items in place.
- Execute moves in the background.
- Respect Bungie's transfer rate limits.
- Stop when the vault is full.
- Report what moved successfully.
- Report items that could not be moved.

2. Prepare a dismantling session
- Begin with the ranked Shard List.
- Confirm candidates intended for dismantling.
- Stage confirmed junk onto a selected character in batches.
- Fit as many eligible items as possible into available inventory space.
- Locked items and weapons with at least 500 tracked kills are protected by the documented staging workflow.
- Dismantle items yourself inside Destiny 2.
- Return to the app and use Continue to prepare the next batch.
- Check live inventory state between batches so newly freed slots can be reused.

3. Prepare infusion fuel
- Inspect eligible weapons and armor slots.
- Identify high-power vault items that outperform the currently equipped item.
- Exclude protected or especially valuable categories according to workflow eligibility rules, such as god rolls, keepers, exotics, locked items, and favorites.
- Stage up to two eligible candidates for a slot where available.
- Choose which candidate to consume in-game.

4. Audit your equipped build
- Check equipped armor and weapons.
- Check armor mods.
- Check subclass aspects and fragments.
- Check artifact perks.
- Check exotic synergy.
- Identify potential misconfigurations.
- Provide specific improvements with relevant mod and perk names.
- Report when the setup is already considered optimal.

The build audit evaluates the configuration being worn, not just unwanted stored items.

J. AI LOADOUT BUILDER

The AI loadout builder accepts a natural-language description and proposes a configuration based on gear the player owns.

Build-generation interactions
- Enter a free-form description of the build.
- Specify an activity, such as a Grandmaster Nightfall.
- Describe a desired element, ability loop, damage resistance, or playstyle.
- Choose or refine an activity using the activity selector.
- Let the system consider activity modifiers.
- Request a build based on gear actually owned.
- Refine the proposed build through follow-up conversation.
- Request alternative weapons or specific weapon characteristics.
- Ask why a build works.
- Save the resulting build as a loadout.

What the builder can select
- Owned weapons.
- Owned exotic armor.
- Armor pieces.
- Subclass.
- Subclass aspects and fragments.
- Armor mods.
- Relevant unlocked artifact perks.
- Equipment with appropriate element and perk synergy.

Build reasoning
- Explain why particular items were selected.
- Save reasoning with the loadout so it can be revisited.
- Ask follow-up questions such as why a setup works or what its tradeoffs are.

Keep my equipped weapons
- Leave current weapons alone when that option is enabled.
- Focus the proposed build on armor, mods, subclass, and artifact-related choices instead of replacing the weapons already carried.

Applying a build
- Apply a saved loadout with a single action.
- Equip eligible weapons and armor.
- Configure supported subclass and mod settings.
- Handle eligible item moves as needed.
- Display a checklist of what was applied.
- Report missing or problematic items when a saved loadout cannot be applied exactly.
- Repair a loadout when appropriate replacements are available.

Saving, updating, and sharing
- Rename a saved loadout.
- Attach notes.
- Preserve the loadout's identity while updating it from the equipped setup.
- Re-equip a previously saved configuration.
- Save a configuration to an in-game loadout slot.
- Equip supported in-game loadout slots through the app.
- Share a public link that people can open without signing in.
- Render a shared build from a snapshot, retaining the shared version even if the owner later changes their build.

Loadout integrity
- Keep gear used by saved loadouts out of the Shard List.
- Detect when a referenced item is missing or has been dismantled.
- Notify about relevant better replacements after synchronization.
- Help repair a loadout using an eligible replacement.
- Check live item and subclass state during application to reduce errors caused by changes made in-game since the last sync.

Documented loadout support extends beyond basic weapons and armor to include subclass configuration, Ghost and Ghost mods, ship, Sparrow, emblem, weapon shaders, and weapon ornaments.

C. SMART MOVES — AUTOMATIC INVENTORY MANAGEMENT

Smart Moves helps a transfer succeed even if the destination is full. DIM may move other gear out of the way and use the vault as an intermediate location.

Smart Move behaviors
- Automatically make space for incoming gear.
- Move items between a character and the vault.
- Account for full inventory slots.
- Respect the game's transfer pathway through the vault.
- Reserve space when handling several related moves.
- Prefer leaving equipped gear alone unless a move requires otherwise.
- Consider recently moved items when deciding what to shuffle.
- Consider in-game loadouts when deciding which pieces to preserve.
- Use tags to influence destinations.
- Prefer keeping appropriate gear on compatible characters.
- Prefer moving lower-priority items to the vault when necessary.
- Account for class compatibility.
- Avoid unnecessary movement when a suitable destination already has space.

Smart Moves is not a random shuffle system. Its rules are intended to preserve useful gear and minimize the disruption caused by moving other items. Tags help express intent: archived items should stay stored, while infusion items may need to be available for power upgrades.

E. ITEM TAGS AND NOTES

Official documentation: https://github.com/DestinyItemManager/DIM/wiki/Tags-and-Notes

DIM provides five built-in tags. Each item can have one of these tags; the tags are not user-renamable.

- Favorite: specially valued gear; tends to remain where you put it.
- Keep: gear you intend to retain; prefers remaining on the character.
- Junk: gear intended for deletion; tends to move to a character for deletion.
- Infuse: gear intended for infusion; tends to move to the vault.
- Archive: gear worth keeping but not actively using; tends to stay in the vault.

Tagging interactions
- Open an item's popup and assign a tag.

- Clear a tag.

- Use tags to influence Smart Moves when inventories are crowded.

Notes

THE MOST IMPORTANT FUNCTIONAL DIFFERENCES

1. Deciding what to keep
Shard or Keep evaluates the actual collection, grades rolls, ranks competing copies, evaluates Armor 3.0 redundancy, and turns its results into a prioritized Shard List. Its AI loadout builder can propose a configuration based on items the player actually owns.

DIM gives flexible tools for inspecting and comparing the same equipment, especially search, wishlist indicators, Compare, and Organizer. Its approach is more centered on giving players detailed tools to make the decision themselves.

2. Building an optimized loadout
Both are capable in different ways. Shard or Keep takes a natural-language request and proposes a complete build based on owned equipment. DIM has a detailed optimizer that allows users to specify stat priorities, constraints, mods, exotics, eligible armor, and subclass configuration. DIM is particularly strong when precise control over equipment selection is needed.

3. Managing a huge inventory
DIM offers multiple ways to work with the same collection: search queries, tags, notes, Item Feed, Compare, Organizer, CSV operations, Smart Moves, and Farming Mode. Shard or Keep approaches the problem through appraisals, the ranked Shard List, bulk actions, and loadout protection.

4. Understanding what is left to do
Shard or Keep provides dedicated Content Explorer, catalyst, triumph, title, and completion-planning pages. DIM has Progress and Records, along with quest, rank, collection, and objective information. The systems organize this information differently.

IMPORTANT BOUNDARIES AND CAVEATS

Shard or Keep
- It cannot dismantle gear through Bungie's API.
- Appraisals are recommendations based on available game data and community wishlists, not an absolute guarantee that a roll is ideal for every player.
- The loadout builder uses synchronized gear; it cannot equip an item the player does not have.
- Certain artifact changes must still be performed inside Destiny 2.
- Accurate current information depends on synchronization and Bungie's service availability.

DIM
- The armor optimizer is a calculation tool, not an in-game armor-upgrade or resource-spending mechanism.
- The Infusion Fuel Finder does not carry out infusion.
- Searches only support attributes and filter syntax implemented by DIM.
- Wishlist recommendations depend on the selected wishlist and its available content.
- Equipment transfers can require several underlying moves, even when initiated through one action.
- Vendor, purchase, and equipment operations depend on Bungie's service capabilities and state.
- Offline mode does not mean all live game actions work without a server connection.
